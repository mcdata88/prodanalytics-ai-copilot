## Top 3 Revenue Drivers — Voice of Customer

### 1. RBAC / SSO Granularity ($12.42M Impacted ARR)
- **Customer Pain:** Enterprise security and IT teams are holding back wider rollout and new seats because the only roles are admin and read-only. They need SCIM provisioning, SSO group-to-role mapping, and access limits by team or business unit, including keeping contractors away from logs that contain PII. These requests come in against all four products.
- **Representative Quote:** *"Audit requires us to restrict access to production data by business unit. Current roles are too coarse."* (`TKT-1030`, Enterprise, $2.51M ARR)
- **PM Recommendation:** Ship scoped custom roles (permissions by team, service and data type) together with SCIM and SSO group mapping. Build it once at the platform level, not separately in each product. That removes a barrier to seat expansion across $12.4M of ARR, and $3.83M of that ARR renews within 90 days.

### 2. OpenTelemetry Native Ingestion ($11.42M Impacted ARR)
- **Customer Pain:** Platform teams are standardizing on OpenTelemetry and refuse to deploy another proprietary agent. When OTLP data goes through the vendor collector today, it loses resource attributes.
- **Representative Quote:** *"We are standardising on OpenTelemetry. Please support native OTLP ingestion without the proprietary agent."* (`TKT-1019`, Enterprise, $2.49M ARR)
- **PM Recommendation:** Build native OTLP endpoints for traces and metrics that keep OTel semantic conventions intact. Only $0.07M of this ARR renews in the next 90 days, so this is a bet on **expansion**, mainly APM attach, not on retention. Schedule it after the themes with heavy renewal exposure.

### 3. Compliance Framework Coverage ($8.85M Impacted ARR)
- **Customer Pain:** Auditors ask for Cloud Security posture findings mapped to CIS, SOC 2, PCI and HIPAA. Some customers also need custom frameworks that match their own internal controls.
- **Representative Quote:** *"Please add CIS and SOC 2 benchmark packs to Cloud Security posture checks - auditors keep asking for mapped evidence."* (`TKT-1049`, Enterprise, $2.30M ARR)
- **PM Recommendation:** Prioritize exportable compliance reports mapped to frameworks: CIS and SOC 2 first, then PCI and HIPAA. This theme has the **largest 90-day renewal exposure, $4.89M**. It also lines up with this week's +8.45% growth in Cloud Security Enterprise ingestion (see the WBR).

## Method & Caveats
- **Classification:** The ordered regex taxonomy in the skill classified all 92 in-scope tickets, so **0** needed judgment. Only 3 tickets ended up Unclassified, and all 3 are out-of-scope `question` tickets: TKT-1012 (API key rotation), TKT-1076 (Terraform provider) and TKT-1097 (agent changelog). They are separate how-to requests with no common theme, so **no taxonomy addition is proposed**. [Q3]
- **Scope gap:** 5 `bug` tickets fall outside the skill's scope because it ranks only `feature_request` and `complaint`. All 5 are dashboard slowness or timeouts (TKT-1004, -1033, -1035, -1036, -1072). If they were included, Dashboard Performance would double from 5 to 10 tickets, so the theme is **understated** here. It is worth routing to the APM / Infra Monitoring engineering backlog separately. [Q3]
- **Ranking sensitivity** [Q4]:

  | Theme | Rank by ARR | by 90-day renewal ARR | by tickets | by at-risk accounts |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | RBAC / SSO | 1 | 2 | 3 | 3 (tie) |
  | OpenTelemetry | 2 | 6 | 4 | 3 (tie) |
  | Compliance | 3 | **1** | 5 | 3 (tie) |
  | Log Overage & Retention | 4 | 3 | **1** | **1** |
  | Alert Noise | 5 | 5 | 2 | 2 |
  | Cost Attribution | 6 | 4 | 6 | 3 (tie) |
  | Dashboard Performance | 7 | 7 | 7 | 3 (tie) |

  **How to read this:** RBAC and OTel are the largest long-term revenue levers. Compliance carries the most near-term renewal ARR. Log Overage is the most urgent retention risk: 16 of its 22 accounts are already At Risk or Critical, and the WBR shows these accounts actively cutting ingestion by −18% WoW. Fixing Log Overage is mostly pricing and packaging (retention tiers, budget caps), which probably costs less engineering than the platform features above. It should run in parallel and not wait its turn in the ARR ranking.
- **Deduplication check** [Q6, Q7]: 56 distinct `current_arr` values are each shared by more than one account across the warehouse. That means `SUM(DISTINCT current_arr)` can silently undercount. In this snapshot it happens to match the deduplicated total for every theme. Summing ARR per ticket instead would **overcount RBAC by $2.51M**, because one Enterprise account filed two RBAC tickets.
- **Data limits:** These demand signals come only from tickets. The warehouse has no sales-call notes, community requests or churn surveys. Synthetic ticket text repeats templates (3–4 distinct texts per theme, 22 for Log Overage), so the quotes illustrate the themes and are not unique customer voices. [Q5]

## Query Appendix

Every query ran through `run_readonly_sql` on the `ipa-analytics` MCP server, in the order shown. `<theme_case>` stands for the 7-branch `CASE` expression written out in full in Q4.

**Q1. `get_metric_definitions()`:** returned `reporting_calendar`. The as-of date is `MAX(week_start) + 7 days` = 2026-11-09.

**Q2. `list_schema()`:** returned 3 tables. `support_tickets` has 100 rows; its columns are ticket_id, account_id, product_name, created_at, ticket_type and raw_text.

**Q3. Theme × ticket type, with the text of out-of-scope and Unclassified tickets (11 rows).** The first attempt was rejected with a binder error because a non-aggregated `CASE` was wrapped around `string_agg`. It was fixed with an aggregate `FILTER` and re-run:
```sql
WITH t AS (SELECT ticket_id, ticket_type, raw_text, <theme_case> AS theme FROM support_tickets)
SELECT theme, ticket_type, COUNT(*) AS n,
  string_agg(ticket_id || ': ' || raw_text, ' | ') FILTER (WHERE theme = 'Unclassified' OR ticket_type NOT IN ('feature_request', 'complaint')) AS out_of_scope_detail
FROM t GROUP BY ALL ORDER BY 1, 2
```

**Q4. Revenue-weighted ranking, with alternative rank orders (7 rows).**
```sql
WITH cal AS (SELECT MAX(week_start) + INTERVAL 7 DAY AS as_of FROM weekly_product_usage),
t AS (
  SELECT ticket_id, account_id, product_name, raw_text,
    CASE
      WHEN regexp_matches(lower(raw_text), 'overage|retention|cold tier|budget cap|drop rules|pre-ingest|log bill') THEN 'Log Overage Pricing & Retention Tiering'
      WHEN regexp_matches(lower(raw_text), 'opentelemetry|otel|otlp') THEN 'OpenTelemetry Native Ingestion'
      WHEN regexp_matches(lower(raw_text), 'cost attribution|chargeback|cost center|cost breakdown|monitoring bill|attribute observability spend') THEN 'Custom Cost Attribution'
      WHEN regexp_matches(lower(raw_text), 'rbac|sso|scim|permission|roles|restrict access') THEN 'RBAC / SSO Granularity'
      WHEN regexp_matches(lower(raw_text), 'compliance|soc 2|pci|hipaa|cis |auditor') THEN 'Compliance Framework Coverage'
      WHEN regexp_matches(lower(raw_text), 'alert|pager|noise|dedup|threshold') THEN 'Alert Noise Reduction'
      WHEN regexp_matches(lower(raw_text), 'dashboard|timeout|load time|performance') THEN 'Dashboard Performance'
      ELSE 'Unclassified' END AS theme
  FROM support_tickets WHERE ticket_type IN ('feature_request', 'complaint')),
ta AS (SELECT DISTINCT theme, account_id FROM t),
acct AS (
  SELECT ta.theme, a.*,
    (a.renewal_date >= cal.as_of AND a.renewal_date < cal.as_of + INTERVAL 90 DAY) AS renew90
  FROM ta JOIN accounts a USING (account_id), cal),
arr AS (
  SELECT theme, COUNT(*) AS accounts, SUM(current_arr) AS impacted_arr,
    COALESCE(SUM(current_arr) FILTER (WHERE renew90), 0) AS renewal_arr_90d,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE health_status IN ('At Risk', 'Critical')) AS at_risk_accts
  FROM acct GROUP BY theme),
seg AS (
  SELECT theme, arg_max(arr_tier, tier_arr) AS top_segment
  FROM (SELECT theme, arr_tier, SUM(current_arr) AS tier_arr FROM acct GROUP BY ALL) GROUP BY theme),
prod AS (SELECT theme, mode(product_name) AS product_area, COUNT(*) AS tickets, COUNT(DISTINCT product_name) AS products FROM t GROUP BY theme)
SELECT RANK() OVER (ORDER BY impacted_arr DESC, renewal_arr_90d DESC) AS rank_arr,
  RANK() OVER (ORDER BY renewal_arr_90d DESC) AS rank_renewal,
  RANK() OVER (ORDER BY tickets DESC) AS rank_tickets,
  RANK() OVER (ORDER BY at_risk_accts DESC) AS rank_at_risk,
  arr.theme, product_area, products, tickets, accounts, impacted_arr, renewal_arr_90d, at_risk_accts, top_segment
FROM arr JOIN seg USING (theme) JOIN prod USING (theme)
ORDER BY rank_arr
```

**Q5. Representative quote per theme, taken from the highest-ARR account (7 rows).**
```sql
WITH t AS (SELECT ticket_id, account_id, raw_text, <theme_case> AS theme
           FROM support_tickets WHERE ticket_type IN ('feature_request', 'complaint'))
SELECT theme, arg_max(ticket_id, a.current_arr) AS ticket_id, arg_max(a.arr_tier, a.current_arr) AS arr_tier,
       MAX(a.current_arr) AS arr, arg_max(raw_text, a.current_arr) AS quote,
       COUNT(DISTINCT raw_text) AS distinct_texts
FROM t JOIN accounts a USING (account_id) GROUP BY theme ORDER BY arr DESC
```

**Q6. Deduplicated ARR compared with `SUM(DISTINCT)` and with a per-ticket sum (7 rows).**
```sql
WITH t AS (SELECT account_id, <theme_case> AS theme
           FROM support_tickets WHERE ticket_type IN ('feature_request', 'complaint')),
dedup AS (SELECT theme, SUM(a.current_arr) AS correct_arr
          FROM (SELECT DISTINCT theme, account_id FROM t) d JOIN accounts a USING (account_id) GROUP BY theme),
naive AS (SELECT theme, SUM(DISTINCT a.current_arr) AS naive_distinct_arr, SUM(a.current_arr) AS naive_ticket_arr
          FROM t JOIN accounts a USING (account_id) GROUP BY theme)
SELECT theme, correct_arr, naive_distinct_arr, naive_ticket_arr,
  correct_arr - naive_distinct_arr AS distinct_undercount, naive_ticket_arr - correct_arr AS ticket_overcount
FROM dedup JOIN naive USING (theme) ORDER BY correct_arr DESC
```

**Q7. Scope size and number of shared ARR values (1 row).**
```sql
SELECT COUNT(*) AS tickets, COUNT(DISTINCT account_id) AS accounts,
  (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM support_tickets) AS all_tickets,
  (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM (SELECT current_arr FROM accounts GROUP BY 1 HAVING COUNT(*) > 1)) AS shared_arr_values
FROM support_tickets WHERE ticket_type IN ('feature_request', 'complaint')
```
